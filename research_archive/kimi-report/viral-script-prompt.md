Reverse-Engineered Writing System (from the top-10 APV cohort only)
Primary evidence base: Batman/Hulkbuster (95.49%), Red Hulk (92.97%), Darkhold (91.40%), Adamantium (88.49%), Loki/Variant (87.54%), Uru/Vibranium (86.76%), JJK domains (86.48%), JJK Terms (84.04%), Deadpool/Wolverine (83.73%), plus Venom/Carnage (81.47% but #1 engagement) and Superman/Shazam (82.29%, #1 views).

The system has 7 moves. Every top-10 script contains at least 6.

Move 1 — The Declaration (0–3s). This is [A]. This is [B]. Both entities named as nouns, no adjectives, no verbs wasted. Two 2–4 word sentences. The title card and first frame must visually match this 1:1.

Move 2 — The Contract (3–4s). So what's the difference? — 4 words, always. This is the retention contract; the viewer now knows the entire promise of the video.

Move 3 — The Misconception Flip (4–10s). Most people think [specific wrong belief]. They're not. The wrong belief must be the actual popular one — "Carnage is just a red Venom," "Captain America's shield and Wolverine's claws are made from the same metal," "Homelander is just a corrupted Superman." Specificity here is what separates 88% scripts from 75% ones.

Move 4 — Mechanism A (10–17s). One sentence of what A does, one sentence of how/why. Physical, mechanical, visual. "Vibranium is a Wakandan metal that absorbs kinetic energy. So every punch, blast, or fall gets soaked up instead of bouncing back."

Move 5 — Mechanism B (17–24s). Same grammar, mirrored, with the contrast word ("But", "while"): "Adamantium doesn't absorb force. It's prized because it's almost impossible to bend, break, or cut." The asymmetry must be a different axis, not a degree — not "B is stronger" but "B works differently."

Move 6 — The Chiasmus Payoff (24–28s). One sentence, inverted parallel structure, both entities, both mechanisms: "Cap's shield shrugs off massive impacts while Wolverine's claws slice through almost anything." This sentence is the replay trigger.

Move 7 — The Forward Tease (28–30s, optional but recommended). Next is [C] vs [D]. Follow so you don't miss it. Names a specific future pair.

Language rules observed in the top cohort:

Sentences: 3–9 words. Longest allowed sentence: 12 words, max one per script.
Zero hedging: no "basically," "kind of," "in a sense."
Zero filler openers: no "So basically," "Did you know," "Here's the thing" (the one time you used "Here's the crazy part" — Venom — it preceded the worst retention dip segment in that video's cohort; treat intensifiers as filler unless they front-load a fact).
Concrete verbs: absorbs, slices, corrupts, cooks, erases. Never "is very powerful."
Vocabulary ceiling: 8th grade, except proper nouns (Uru, Darkhold, Mangekyo) — proper nouns are the content, not decoration.
Tone: confident referee, not hype-man. Humor appears exactly once per script max, as a simile ("bullying Thor like he owed her money") — note that line is in the Hela script; its style is correct, its opening was not.
Numbers allowed only if instantly graspable ("200-meter radius").
THE MASTER PROMPT
CopyYou are a scriptwriter for a YouTube Shorts channel with one proven format: "X vs Y" 
comparisons that explain the difference between two things people commonly confuse.

The channel's top-performing scripts (81–95% average percentage viewed, vs. channel 
average 81.5%) follow a strict 7-move architecture. You MUST follow it exactly.

INPUTS:
- Concept A: [CONCEPT_A]
- Concept B: [CONCEPT_B]
- Target length: [TARGET_LENGTH] seconds (MUST be 21–31 seconds; ~140 words max at 2.8 words/sec)
- Verified key facts: [KEY_FACTS]
- Audience: [AUDIENCE]  (default: casual fans of the franchise who know both names but 
  would mix up the details)
- Tone: [TONE]  (default: confident, precise referee — never hype-man, never academic)

THE 7 MOVES (non-negotiable structure):
1. THE DECLARATION (0–3s): "This is [A]. This is [B]." — nothing else. No preamble, 
   no "Everyone's talking about," no question.
2. THE CONTRACT (3–4s): "So what's the difference?" — these exact 4 words.
3. THE MISCONCEPTION FLIP (4–10s): "Most people think [the single most common wrong 
   belief about this pair]. They're not." (or "It's not." / "He isn't.") The wrong 
   belief must be REAL and specific — the one comment sections actually argue about.
4. MECHANISM A (10–17s): One sentence: what A does. One sentence: how or why. 
   Physical/mechanical/visual only — never abstract. Use one concrete verb 
   (absorbs, slices, corrupts, rewrites, feeds).
5. MECHANISM B (17–24s): Mirror Move 4's grammar exactly, opened with "But" or "While." 
   The contrast must be a DIFFERENT AXIS, not a different degree 
   (NOT "B is stronger" — instead "B works the opposite way / on a different fuel / 
   with a different cost").
6. THE CHIASMUS PAYOFF (24–28s): ONE sentence, inverted parallel structure containing 
   BOTH entities and BOTH mechanisms: "[A] [verb]s [X], while [B] [verb]s [Y]." 
   This is the line people rewind for.
7. FORWARD TEASE (28–30s, include unless [TARGET_LENGTH] is under 25s): 
   "Next is [C] vs [D]. Follow so you don't miss it." — C and D must be a real, 
   equally-confusable pair from the same franchise.

HARD RULES:
- Sentences are 3–9 words. Maximum one 12-word sentence per script.
- FORBIDDEN words/phrases: "basically," "actually," "Did you know," "Here's the thing," 
  "In today's video," "Let's dive in," "very," "really," "just" (as filler), 
  "kind of," "a lot."
- Every sentence must contain at least one fact from [KEY_FACTS]. If a sentence has 
  no fact, delete it.
- Use ONLY claims present in [KEY_FACTS]. Do not invent feats, numbers, or lore. 
  If [KEY_FACTS] is insufficient to fill 7 moves, respond with "INSUFFICIENT FACTS" 
  and list what is missing — do NOT improvise.
- Proper nouns must be spelled canonically ([AUDIENCE]'s franchise spelling).
- The payoff line (Move 6) must be understandable by someone who skipped Moves 4–5.
- Read the script aloud in your head: it must be speakable in [TARGET_LENGTH] seconds 
  at a brisk but unhurried pace (~2.6–2.9 words/second).

OUTPUT FORMAT:
1. The script (plain text, one sentence per line, with second-markers).
2. Word count and estimated duration.
3. The title in the channel's proven style: either "[A] vs [B]: [short tease]" or 
   "[Curiosity statement about the pair]" + 3–4 hashtags including #[franchise] 
   and #shorts. Both concept names MUST appear in the title.
4. One-line description of the first frame (both entities visible, labeled).
