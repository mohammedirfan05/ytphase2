A. What's Working
The Hook
Observed: 8 of your 10 highest-APV Shorts open with the identical architecture — This is [A]. This is [B]. So what's the difference? — delivered inside the first 3 seconds. These 8 averaged 87.0% APV vs. 73.9% for everything else. Examples with metrics:

Batman/Hulkbuster (95.49% APV): "This is Hellbat Armor. This is Hulkbuster. So what's the difference?"
Red Hulk (92.97%): "This is Hulk. This is Red Hulk. So what's the difference?"
Adamantium (88.49%): "This is vibranium. This is adamantium. So what's the difference?"
Why it works (inference, supported): It does three things in ~4 seconds — names both entities (familiarity hit → recognition dopamine), promises a payoff (the difference), and stakes the video's entire scope (viewer can instantly judge "I want this answer"). There is zero throat-clearing.

The Misconception Bridge
Observed: The single highest-leverage mid-script line in your catalog is the pattern Most people think [wrong belief]. They're not./It's not. It appears in Adamantium (88.49%), Uru/Vibranium (86.76%), Darkhold (91.40%), JJK domains (86.48%), Superman/Shazam-tier scripts, and Deadpool/Wolverine (83.73%). Median APV of videos containing an explicit misconception flip: ~85%; of those without: ~73% (n=32; treat as strong correlation, not causation).

Why it matters: this line is your second hook, placed at ~seconds 6–10 — exactly where Shorts retention curves normally cliff. It re-opens the loop right when the first loop closes.

The Parallel Payoff (ending symmetry)
Observed: The top tier ends with a mirrored one-line synthesis that inverts the opening: "Vibranium stops physical attacks while Uru absorbs mystical power." / "Superman is powered by the sun, while Shazam is powered by magic." / "Thor wins fights. Odin decides who gets the power to fight." Every video above 86% APV has this exact chiastic ending (A-does-X while B-does-Y). It costs 2–3 seconds and delivers the retention and the satisfaction simultaneously — likely driving both the 72.6% "stayed to watch" rate and replay (which is how several APVs exceed 90%).

Pacing / Information Density
Observed: Top performers run ~2.6–3.0 words/second with sentences of 4–9 words, one new fact per sentence, zero filler. Red Hulk (92.97%, 23s) delivers 6 distinct facts in 63 words. Compare: War Machine/Iron Man (64.43%, 42s) spends 8 sentences and a trivia digression ("In almost every comic-sparring match...") to make one point — its density is ~1.9 facts/10s vs. Red Hulk's ~2.6, and its APV is 28.5 points lower.

Topic
Observed: Winning topics share four traits, all quantifiable in your data:

Two famous entities (both named characters/objects the average viewer recognizes: Hulk, Wolverine, Thor, Venom, Superman, Batman, Iron Man) — top-10-by-views are 100% household-name Marvel/DC.
A physical/mechanical distinction (absorbs kinetic energy vs. doesn't; gets stronger vs. gets hotter; forged for the stones vs. emergency copy) — not abstract/semantic ones.
An existing "same thing?" confusion — Vibranium/Adamantium, Venom/Carnage, Hulk/Red Hulk, Mjolnir-adjacent metals. These have pre-built search + comment-section argument energy (Venom: 836 likes, best engagement ratio on the channel at 3.5% likes/views).
Franchise freshness: JJK Terms (24.5k views) rode JJK's anime visibility; your Marvel metals pair (Uru/Vibranium, 22k) and Adamantium (24.6k) both capitalized on the metal-confusion cluster — note you published two metals videos 3 days apart and both hit, suggesting cluster cannibalization is not yet a problem at your scale.
B. What's Hurting Performance
B1. Length without density (the 37s+ wall). Every video ≥37s underperformed: Sharingan/Rinnegan (38s, 59.14%), Poison/Venom 3-pair (40s, 65.40%), War Machine (42s, 64.43%), Doom/Iron Man (45s, 87.30% APV but only 2,651 views — retained well, failed distribution, likely because a 45s Short's swipe-through economics hurt its feed trial), Crime Terms (72s, 43.70% — channel's worst APV). Observed: the algorithm is not rewarding you for longer watch time; it's punishing lower completion rate.

B2. Question-first and context-first openings. "Who's stronger? Hella or Thanos?" (immature, but scraped retention signals weak), "Everyone's talking about Dr. Doom, but they are missing the biggest difference" (Doom/Iron Man: 2,651 views — the slowest-distributing Marvel video despite 87.3% APV), "What's the difference?" as a title with no entities (3,460 views on a 3-pair video). Inference: openings that delay naming both entities past ~2 seconds lose the recognition hit; titles without both entity names lose the search/feed click signal. "What's the difference?" is also your only untitled-by-content video — and it shows.

B3. Off-cluster topics. AI/ML (352 views, 56.84%), Rich/Wealthy (283, 67.53%), Virus/Bacteria (1,674), Crime Terms (1,171), CGI/VFX (2,634). Observed: these 5 average ~1,500 views vs. ~14,000 median for Marvel/DC. The CGI/VFX case is diagnostic: 64.08% APV with solid production — it didn't fail on craft, it failed on audience mismatch. Your feed audience is fandom; a filmmaker-vocabulary video was shown to superhero fans and swiped.

B4. Repetitive template fatigue inside multi-pair videos. The 3-pair and 6-pair videos repeat "So what's the difference?" 3–6 times. Crime Terms (43.70% APV) is the extreme: by pair #3 the phrase becomes a metronome predicting the exit point. JJK Terms survived it (84.04%) only because the content was fandom-terminology with genuine confusion value.

B5. Truncated/failed endings. The Deadpool/Wolverine transcript cuts off mid-word ("Regen-") at 23s — still hit 83.73% APV on strength of topic, but gained only 2 subs on 4,486 views. The 99% Marvel Fans (Spiderverse) video opens mid-thought ("But this is the spiderverse...") — 75.36% on a strong topic. Sloppy edges are costing you the last 5–10 APV points and subscriber conversion.

B6. CTA placement is fine; CTA sameness is not. "Follow for more" closes ~20 videos. The two highest sub-per-view videos used content-forward CTAs ("Next is Dr. Strange vs. Scarlet Witch" — Odin/Thor, 41 subs; "Subscribe because the next pair is worse" — Red Hulk, 25 subs on 19.5k views in ~1 day, the best sub velocity on the channel). Weak signal, but directionally consistent.
